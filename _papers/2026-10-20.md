## 1. [LoCA: Local Credit Assignment for Reinforcement Learning of Autoregressive Flow-Matching TTS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28035)

**Authors:** Jie Liu, Junyu Zhou, Junhao Zhong, WeiSen Tang, Rukai Cai et al.

**Categories:** `cs.SD`

> Reinforcement learning can improve text-to-speech synthesis through feedback on generated speech. However, sentence-level rewards do not identify local errors, while signed regression updates can lead to excessive fitting or repulsion of sampled outputs. We propose LoCA (Local Credit Assignment) for autoregressive flow-matching text-to-speech. LoCA uses ASR-based text alignment to locate content errors in generated speech and redistribute sentence-level advantages across acoustic frames. To improve training stability, we introduce reference-relative positive and negative thresholds that limit continued fitting and repulsion. Experiments on VoxCPM2 show that LoCA reduces sample-weighted content error on a multilingual CV3 evaluation subset by 20.5% and 14.3% relative to the pretrained backbone and GROW, respectively, while improving speaker similarity. LoCA also improves content accuracy on the Seed-TTS-Eval hard subset. Ablation results support the contributions of local credit and threshold-based regression control.

## 2. [StyleStream 2.0: Fast and Controllable Streaming Voice Style Conversion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28940)

**Authors:** Yisi Liu, Nicholas Lee, Gopala K. Anumanchipalli

**Categories:** `cs.SD, cs.AI`
  | accepted to NeurIPS 2026

> Voice style conversion (VSC) aims to transform a source utterance to match the timbre, accent, and emotion of a target voice while preserving linguistic content. StyleStream 1.0 introduced the first streamable zero-shot VSC system with state-of-the-art conversion quality, but remained limited in two ways: approximately 1s end-to-end latency on consumer hardware, and the lack of a text-based interface for designing or editing target voices. We present StyleStream 2.0, a fast and controllable streaming VSC framework that addresses these limitations. To reduce latency, we train a few-step pixel MeanFlow model and further fine-tune it with autoregressive feedback inspired by Self Forcing, improving robustness under streaming inference. This reduces end-to-end latency to 520~ms on a consumer GPU, a 2x speedup over StyleStream 1.0, while maintaining conversion quality. To enable controllable style manipulation, we remove the mel-spectrogram context and instead route target style information through a compact style embedding. In this embedding space, we train a unified text-conditioned flow matching model that supports both text-based voice design, which maps natural language prompts to voice styles, and instruction-based voice editing, which modifies a specific attribute of a source utterance while preserving the rest. Experiments show that StyleStream 2.0 achieves the strongest target style fidelity on VSC, voice design, and voice editing, while remaining competitive on intelligibility and non-edited attribute preservation.

## 3. [Qwen-Audio-3.1-ASR Technical Report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28711)

**Authors:** Chuanmeng Bian, Daren Chen, Peixin Chen, Zhigao Chen, Chong Deng et al.

**Categories:** `cs.SD, cs.CL`
  | 31 pages. Authors are listed in alphabetical order of last name. Project page: https://qwenaudio.github.io/qwen-audio-3.1-asr/

> We present Qwen-Audio-3.1-ASR, a family of audio-language models that builds upon its predecesor Qwen-Audio-3.0-ASR with improved speech recognition and expanded capabilities in speaker-diarized transcription and general audio understanding. The family retains support for 30 languages and 16 Chinese dialects, together with instruction-controlled capabilities including historical-context conditioning, hierarchical hotword customization, and native transcription polishing. Speaker-diarized ASR produces structured outputs containing speaker labels, timestamps, and recognized text, maintaining consistent speaker labels across turns and the representation of overlapping speech. The general audio understanding capabilities cover audio-, speech-, and music-captioning, temporal grounding and sound event detection, as well as audio question answering and multiple-choice reasoning. These tasks broaden the application scope from recognizing spoken words to interpreting speakers, acoustic events, and their temporal relations. To accommodate different deployment requirements, the family includes variants for asynchronous file transcription, low-latency streaming, interactive voice input, and non-streaming recognition, with speaker-diarized transcription and general audio understanding available in certain variants. Evaluations span conventional ASR, multilingual and dialectal speech, speaker-diarized transcription, and general audio understanding, demonstrating the capabilities of the Qwen-Audio-3.1-ASR family across transcription and broader audio analysis tasks.

## 4. [Uncovering Audio-Text Alignment in Large Audio-Language Models through Cross-Modal Concept Neurons](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28189)

**Authors:** Tzu-Quan Lin, Chih-Kai Yang, Yi-Cheng Lin, Hao Tang, Hung-yi Lee

**Categories:** `cs.SD, cs.CL, eess.AS`

> While the language model backbone is pretrained on text alone, large audio-language models (LALMs) can, to a great extent, recognize acoustic attributes, such as emotion, speaker gender, and spoken language. However, it is unclear how the audio representation is integrated, if at all, with the other text representation before the model produces text. We hypothesize that there is a shared subspace where the audio and text representations are aligned. To test this hypothesis, we identify cross-modal neurons that are responsible for representing both audio and text. We find alignment between audio and text neurons to various degrees across three LALMs and four acoustic attribute types. Causal interventions, steering and deactivating the neurons, confirm that the cross-modal neurons are not just necessary for prediction but can also be manipulated.
