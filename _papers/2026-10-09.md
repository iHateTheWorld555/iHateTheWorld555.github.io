## 1. [Emo-BPO: Emotion Bidirectional Preference Optimization for Diffusion-based Emotional TTS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12958)

**Authors:** Jiacheng Shi, Hongfei Du, Xinyuan Song, Y. Alicia Hong, Yanfu Zhang et al.

**Categories:** `cs.SD, eess.AS`
  | Accepted to Interspeech 2026

> Diffusion-based TTS has demonstrated strong capability in emotional speech generation. However, existing emotion alignment methods primarily reinforce target emotional expressions while overlooking modeling of competing emotional modes, limiting fine-grained controllability. This issue becomes more pronounced under classifier-free guidance (CFG), which relies on contrasting conditional trajectories to shape generation. We propose Emotion Bidirectional Preference Optimization (Emo-BPO), a simple effective framework that jointly learns emotion-aligned and emotion-contrastive score functions from reordered same-text emotional pairs. Our method requires neither auxiliary reward models nor additional annotations, and can be seamlessly integrated into existing diffusion-based TTS systems to improve emotional controllability and expressiveness.

## 2. [SWEAR: Section-Wise Efficient Audio Reasoning via Self-Rated Difficulty](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13081)

**Authors:** Yixuan Li, Minghao Lv, Xuenan Xu, Zihao Zheng, Mengyue Wu

**Categories:** `cs.AI`

> Recent work on large audio-language models (LALMs) explores adaptive reasoning to balance accuracy and efficiency. However, existing approaches often rely on coarse-grained difficulty estimation, overlooking the diverse factors that determine reasoning effort. We address this limitation by deriving five-dimensional difficulty scores and compressed reasoning traces from complementary offline assessments. Based on this characterization, we propose SWEAR, a difficulty-aware reasoning framework that learns difficulty signals during supervised fine-tuning and uses reinforcement learning to adapt reasoning allocation across reasoning stages. Experiments on MMAU-test-mini and MMAR show that SWEAR improves accuracy while substantially reducing generated tokens, shortening reasoning for easy instances without sacrificing performance on difficult ones.
